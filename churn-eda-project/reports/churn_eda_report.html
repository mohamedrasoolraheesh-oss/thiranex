What drives customer churn?

An exploratory analysis of 2,000 synthetic telecom customers: what the data looks like, where churn concentrates, and which factors matter most.

1. Objective and dataset

Goal: find which customer attributes are linked to churn, so retention effort can be aimed at the right people. The dataset was generated for this exercise (random seed 42), so it mimics realistic telecom behaviour but is not real customer data.

2. Data quality

  • Missing values: age has 70 blanks (3.5%) and total_charges has 45 (2.3%). Both are small and appear random, so median imputation is reasonable.
  • Duplicates: none found.
  • Outliers: monthly charges range from 15 to 107 and tenure from 1 to 72. Both are plausible, so nothing was removed.
  • Class balance: 31.6% churn versus 68.4% retained. Moderately imbalanced, which matters if you model this later.

Absolute values ×100. Tenure, total charges are negative (longer or bigger accounts churn less); tickets, charges and age are positive.

Ranked by how strongly they separate churners from stayers:

  1. Contract type: a 44-point gap between the best and worst group.
  2. Tenure: 38 points from the first year to year four and beyond.
  3. Support tickets: a gap of about 30 points from zero tickets to four or more.
  4. Internet service and monthly price: fiber and higher bills go with more churn.
  5. Autopay: small but consistent effect.
  6. Region and age: negligible.

6. Limitations

  • The data is synthetic, so the patterns reflect how it was generated. Real data would be noisier and could show different drivers.
  • Correlation is not causation. Fiber customers might churn because of price, outages or competitor offers, and this data cannot tell which.
  • Tenure and total charges overlap, since total is roughly tenure times monthly price. Treat them as one signal.

7. Conclusions and next steps

  • Offer discounts or perks to move month-to-month customers onto one- or two-year plans, starting with fiber users.
  • Run an onboarding programme for the first 12 months, when churn is highest.
  • Flag customers with three or more tickets for a proactive follow-up call.
  • Encourage autopay sign-up.
  • Next analysis: a logistic regression or gradient-boosted model to quantify each factor while controlling for the others, then test retention offers.
